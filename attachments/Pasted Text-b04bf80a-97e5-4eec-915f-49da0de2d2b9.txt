1. Why a Trust Score — the Honesty Check
Auctum’s brand promise is “Verified, From Origin.” Cup score, ESG, and logistics metrics already live on every lot card. What is missing is a durable, portable honesty signal that answers: “How much should I trust the documents, claims, and counterparties behind this lot or this profile?”
The Trust Score is that signal. It is a continuous 0–100 metric (displayed as an integer with one optional decimal) that:
Attaches permanently to every tier profile — Roaster, Supplier/Exporter, Counterparty, and (derived) Lot.
Is updated by verifiable actions (OCR-accepted documents, on-time payments, QC disputes resolved, audit history), never by self-reported claims alone.
Surfaces as a first-class badge and bar alongside Cup Score and ESG on lot cards, profile headers, and contract review screens.
Provides the checks-and-balances layer: high Trust unlocks faster settlement and lower friction; low Trust forces extra document gates and human review.
2. Trust Score Model
2.1 Definition & Range
Trust Score ∈ [0, 100]. Default for a brand-new counterparty with zero history: 50 (neutral). Floor is 0; ceiling is 100. Displayed in JetBrains Mono (tabular-nums) exactly like cup scores and prices.

2.4 Attachment to Tier Profiles
Every profile that participates in commerce carries its own Trust Score:
Roaster (CRM aggregate) — Trust travels with the account across campaigns, sample kits, and orders.
Supplier / Exporter / Counterparty — Trust is the primary honesty signal shown to buyers.
Lot (derived) — Lot Trust is a blend of the supplier’s current Trust + the verification density of documents attached to that specific lot (SCA report accepted, CoO accepted, etc.).
The score is never “reset” by tier upgrades; subscription or commercial tiers may unlock features, but Trust is earned only through evidence.
3. How Document Scanner / OCR Feeds Trust
The existing services/docintake pipeline (Claude vision + structured outputs, per-field confidence, human accept required) is the primary evidence engine for the Document Verification component (35 %).
On documents.acceptOcr (or equivalent) with a successful link to a lot or counterparty:
Increment document-verification sub-score based on document type weight (SCA lab report and signed contract > packing list).
Cross-check extracted fields against existing lot data; large discrepancies lower Quality Consistency.
Emit domain event trust.evidence_recorded with documentId, entityType, entityId, delta, modelVersion.
Rejected or low-confidence documents that are never accepted do not move Trust (no negative hit for simply uploading a blurry photo).
Repeated contradictions (e.g., claimed cup score 88 vs accepted lab report 81.5) apply a negative delta to Quality Consistency and, if severe, to overall Trust.
Nothing auto-commits: the same human-in-the-loop rule that protects the ledger also protects Trust integrity.

4.1 Primary entry points (when)
Lot Detail / Lot Card expanded view — “Attach evidence” or “Scan lab report / contract” action in the document strip.
QC Audit workspace — primary capture surface for SCA / Q reports (camera or file).
Contract / Offer creation flow — “Scan offer sheet or signed contract” as the first step instead of blank form.
Counterparty / Supplier profile — “Upload verification documents” in the Trust panel.
Warehouse / Receiving — scan warehouse receipt or packing list on goods-in.
Mobile-first field use — camera capture optimized for exporters and QC staff on origin or at port.
4.2 Secondary / contextual
Navigator results — subtle “Needs verification” chip when a lot has low document density; clicking opens the attach flow.
Payment / AR screens — when settling against a contract that still lacks an accepted scanned original, prompt for document before release.
Onboarding of a new supplier — guided Trust-building checklist that includes first OCR upload.
4.3 What is deliberately not a scanner entry
Marketing / campaign screens (COF flows) — keep them clean; Trust is operational.
Public catalog browse for unauthenticated users — Trust badge may be visible, but upload is authenticated only.

0.2 Design gaps — not blocking, but expensive to retrofit
G1 — Aging buckets are unbuildable. ar_aging stores one mutable outstandingSubunits per counterparty. Buckets (0–30 / 31–60 / 61–90 / 90+) require per-invoice dueDate. The Payments/AR page in v1 §8.2 promises buckets the schema cannot produce.
G2 — Push-payment reality is absent. VietQR is a bank transfer. The payer controls the amount and can mistype the memo. v1 models only the happy path: exact amount, correct reference, one payment per intent. No partial payment, overpayment, unmatched payment, duplicate payment, or refund.
G3 — No allocation model. One transfer can settle two invoices; one invoice can take three transfers. There is no join between payment and receivable.
G4 — Foreign exchange is unmodelled. Export contracts price in USD; settlement lands in VND. No rate capture, no realized FX gain/loss, no rule about which currency a domestic contract may use.
G5 — Inbound and outbound money are conflated. partnerPayments (money out to farmers: floor + revenue share) already exists. v1 adds AR (money in from buyers) with no statement of how they relate on a lot's P&L.
G6 — OCR confidence gates business-record creation at ≥0.90. For a cup score, that is an OCR engine setting a farmer's revenue share percentage with no human in the loop.
G7 — Bank details stored in plaintext. counterparties.bankAccountNumber with no encryption, no access log, no retention policy — under Vietnam's PDPD (Decree 13/2023) financial account data is sensitive personal data when the counterparty is an individual farmer.
G8 — Build-time feature flags. VITE_ENABLE_* are inlined at build. You cannot kill a misbehaving payment flow without a redeploy.
G9 — Nine sequential phases, nine agents. Frontend (Phase 6) is blocked on five upstream phases. First demonstrable value lands at the end. No integration risk is retired early.
G10 — Acceptance criteria are not testable. "Idempotency: duplicate webhook does not duplicate records" has no stated observation point or tolerance.

2. Architecture & Design Principles Δ CHANGED
Monorepo, existing app/api/ (Hono + tRPC 11) and app/src/ (React 19 + Vite + Tailwind + shadcn/ui).
Database: MySQL 8 with Drizzle ORM — matching app/drizzle.config.ts. All new tables in app/db/manager-schema.ts and app/db/payments-schema.ts, re-exported from app/db/schema.ts so drizzle-kit picks them up (the config points at a single schema file).
Primary keys: serial(), foreign keys bigint({ mode: "number", unsigned: true }), matching every existing table.
API: tRPC 11 for the application surface. Provider webhooks are plain Hono routes mounted before the tRPC middleware (see §7.2).
Events: a real transactional outbox (see §4). emitEvent() is rewritten to be transaction-aware; side effects move to a consumer.
Money: ISO 4217 minor units. Every monetary column is bigint, always paired with a currency char(3) column. Exponent is derived from the currency, never assumed: VND → 0, USD → 2. formatCents() is deprecated in favour of formatMinor(amountMinor, currency).
Idempotency: scoped, fingerprinted, and response-recording (see §7.3). Not a bare unique index.
Traceability: every payment resolves to an invoice; every invoice line resolves to a coffee_lots.id. This is the invariant an auditor will test.
OCR: proposal-only. The pipeline never creates an approved record. It creates a draft with a sourceDocumentId, and a human with the right role approves it.
2.1 Architecture decision records
ADR-01 — MySQL, not Postgres. Rejected: migrating the platform to Postgres inside this sprint. The existing schema, seeds, and 72 commits of history are MySQL. A dialect migration is a separate, larger project with its own risk budget. Consequence: no jsonb operators; JSON columns use MySQL json type with JSON_EXTRACT for querying, and we index extracted values via generated columns where we need to filter.

ADR-02 — Invoices as the single payable aggregate. Rejected: polymorphic orderId. A payment settles an invoice; an invoice is raised against an order or a contract. This gives one FK target, one place to hold dueDate (which G1 needs), and a natural home for VAT and FX. Consequence: one extra table and an invoice-issuance step; every existing orders row that needs settlement gets an invoice.

ADR-03 — PayOS is the payment rail; Casso is the bank feed. PayOS signs its webhook body (HMAC-SHA256 over the data object's keys sorted alphabetically and joined k=v&k=v, compared against the signature field) and carries a first-class orderCode. Casso authenticates only with a static shared secret in a secure-token header — no payload signature, no timestamp, no replay protection. Anyone who obtains the token can forge a credit. Consequence: Casso events are treated as untrusted notifications: they trigger a re-fetch of the transaction from the Casso API, and only the re-fetched record may move AR. PayOS webhooks may credit directly after signature verification.

ADR-04 — Cup score never enters the payment path from OCR. A revenue-share tier may only be set by a cuppingSessions row that satisfies the existing panel rule. OCR may pre-fill a cupping form; it may not finalise one. Consequence: the "auto-create QC audit at confidence ≥0.9" acceptance criterion in v1 §14.1 is removed.

ADR-05 — Runtime feature flags. Flags are served from the API (config.flags query), cached 30s client-side, with an env-var override for local dev. Consequence: one extra endpoint, and the ability to disable settlement in under a minute without a deploy.

  SUM(CASE WHEN DATEDIFF(CURDATE(), dueAt) <=  0 THEN totalMinor - paidMinor ELSE 0 END) AS current,

  SUM(CASE WHEN DATEDIFF(CURDATE(), dueAt) BETWEEN  1 AND 30 THEN totalMinor - paidMinor ELSE 0 END) AS b30,

3.14 Soft-delete and unique indexes
MySQL has no partial indexes. UNIQUE(invoiceNumber) plus deletedAt means a soft-deleted invoice permanently reserves its number. That is correct for invoice numbers (regulators expect gapless, non-reused sequences) and wrong for things like contractNumber if the business expects reuse. Decide per table, write the decision in a schema comment, and where reuse is required use a generated column: deletedFlag AS (IF(deletedAt IS NULL, 0, id)) STORED and make the unique index (contractNumber, deletedFlag).


4. Events & the Outbox Δ CHANGED
4.1 Rewriting emitEvent()
Current signature ignores transactions and fires rules inline:

// app/api/engine.ts — current

export async function emitEvent(eventType, aggregateType, aggregateId, payload) {

  await getDb().insert(domainEvents).values({...});

  await evaluateRules(eventType, payload);   // side effect, outside any transaction

}

Target:

export async function emitEvent(

  tx: Transaction,                 // REQUIRED — caller's transaction, not a fresh connection

  eventType: string,

  aggregateType: string,

  aggregateId: string | number,

  payload: Payload,

  opts?: { version?: number },

): Promise<number> {                // returns outbox row id

  const [res] = await tx.insert(domainEvents).values({

    eventType, aggregateType, aggregateId: String(aggregateId),

    payload, eventVersion: opts?.version ?? 1,

  });

  return res.insertId;

}

evaluateRules() moves out of emitEvent and into the consumer. This is a breaking change to every existing call site (catalog, orders, samples, CRM, campaigns, warehouse, QC, partners, growth). Do it as a mechanical refactor in its own PR, with the existing e2e flows in app/README.md §"Verified flows" as the regression suite — those fourteen flows are the acceptance test for the refactor. Do not bundle it with new features.

Migration safety: run the consumer and the old inline path side by side behind a flag for one release, with the consumer's rule evaluation writing to a shadow table and a diff job asserting parity, then cut over.
4.2 Consumer
Single worker process, claim-based dispatch, at-least-once delivery.

Poll every 500ms; claim up to 50 rows with FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED.
Handlers are registered by eventType and must be idempotent, keyed on (eventType, aggregateId) or a handler-specific natural key.
Retry with exponential backoff: 1s, 5s, 30s, 2m, 10m, 1h. After 6 attempts move to domain_events_dead and alert.
A handler that throws must not mark the event processed. A handler that decides the event is irrelevant returns skip and the event is marked processed with a skipped reason.
Ordering is per aggregate, not global. Where a handler needs ordering, it checks aggregate state rather than assuming arrival order.

7. Payment Integration Δ CHANGED
This section is the largest rewrite. v1 modelled a card charge. VietQR is a push payment: the payer initiates a bank transfer, chooses the amount, and types (or scans) a memo. Everything below follows from that.
7.1 The memo token
Matching happens on the bank transfer description. Constraints from the field:

Vietnamese banks uppercase, strip diacritics, and truncate memos; PayOS's own description budget is around 25 characters. Casso's reference integration parses a fixed prefix plus digits out of the description.
The token must therefore be short, uppercase, alphanumeric, and unambiguous.

Specification: AUC + 7 characters from Crockford base32 (excludes I, L, O, U) = 10 characters total, derived from the invoice id plus a check character. Stored on invoices.memoToken, unique. Rendered in the QR addInfo field, printed on the invoice, and shown on the payment screen with a copy button.

Matching order:

Exact memoToken found in description (case-insensitive, after stripping non-alphanumerics) → matched.
PayOS orderCode maps to a payment_intents.providerOrderCode → matched.
Exactly one open invoice for a counterparty identified by counterAccountNumber, and the amount matches exactly → matched, flagged matchMethod: 'heuristic' for review.
Otherwise → unmatched or ambiguous; lands in the exception queue.

Never match on amount alone. Two farmers paying the same round number on the same day is not a hypothetical.
7.2 Webhook endpoints Δ CHANGED
Mounted as Hono routes before the tRPC middleware, with express.raw-equivalent body capture:

POST /webhooks/payos

POST /webhooks/casso

Both endpoints:

Capture the raw body before JSON parsing (needed for audit and, for some providers, signature input).
Verify authentication (below). On failure: log with request id, return 401, do not persist as a transaction.
Insert into provider_transactions. Duplicate (provider, providerTxnId) → return 200 immediately, no side effects.
Emit payment.transaction_received in the same transaction.
Return 200 within 2 seconds, before matching runs. Matching happens in the consumer. Providers retry on timeout, and a slow synchronous match is how you get duplicate deliveries.

PayOS. Signature is HMAC-SHA256 over the data object with keys sorted alphabetically and joined as k=v&k=v, compared to the signature field. Use crypto.timingSafeEqual. Note the webhook URL must be registered with PayOS via their confirm-webhook call — add this to the deployment runbook; a forgotten registration means silent non-delivery in production. Verified PayOS transactions may credit AR directly.

Casso. Authentication is a static shared secret in a secure-token header. There is no payload signature, no timestamp, and no replay protection. Treat the callback as an untrusted notification: compare the token in constant time, persist the transaction with signatureValid = false, and then have the consumer re-fetch the transaction from the Casso API before allocating. Only a re-fetched, verifiedAt-stamped record may move money. Additionally, restrict the endpoint by source IP where the provider publishes a range, and rotate the token quarterly.
7.3 Idempotency, properly
A bare UNIQUE(idempotencyKey) returns a duplicate-key error on retry rather than the original result, and cannot distinguish a legitimate retry from key reuse with a different body.

Implement an idempotency_records table: (principalId, key) unique, plus requestFingerprint char(64), status enum('in_flight','completed'), responseSnapshot json, lockedAt, expiresAt (24h).

Same key, same fingerprint, completed → return responseSnapshot, HTTP 200.
Same key, same fingerprint, in-flight → HTTP 409 with Retry-After.
Same key, different fingerprint → HTTP 422 GS-PAY-1001 IdempotencyKeyReuse. Never silently return the old response for a different request.
7.4 Under, over, and unmatched payment
Scenario
Handling
Underpayment
Allocate what arrived. invoices.status = partially_paid. Emit payment.allocated. Remaining balance keeps aging from the original dueAt.
Overpayment
Allocate up to totalMinor. Excess creates a credit: a provider_transactions row with residual unallocated amount, visible in the exception queue for allocation to another invoice or refund. invoices.status = paid; emit invoice.overpaid.
Unmatched
matchStatus = unmatched. Money is real but unassigned — this is a suspense balance and must appear in the AR summary as such, never as zero. Ops resolves via allocations.create.
Duplicate transfer
Distinct providerTxnId, so both persist. The second is unmatched (invoice already paid) and routed to refund. This is correct behaviour, not a bug — do not deduplicate on amount.
Refund / reversal
allocations.reverse writes reversedAt and a reason, recomputes invoices.paidMinor, emits payment.reversed. The outbound transfer itself is manual this sprint and recorded as a note.

7.5 Foreign exchange
When invoice currency ≠ payment currency, payment_allocations.fxRate is captured at allocation time from the counterparty's contract rate (contracts.fxRateLocked) if present, otherwise from a rate source recorded with a timestamp. Realized FX difference is computed and posted to a fx_adjustments ledger — out of scope for the sprint, but the rate must be captured now or the difference is unrecoverable later.

11. Testing Δ CHANGED
11.1 Property-based (fast-check)
Money round-trip: parseMinor(formatMinor(n, c), c) === n for all currencies, all n in bigint range.
No currency mixing: allocating an amount in currency X to an invoice in currency Y without an fxRate throws.
Allocation invariant: for any sequence of allocations and reversals, invoices.paidMinor === SUM(non-reversed allocations) and 0 <= paidMinor.
Unit conversion: gramsToLbs(lbsToGrams(x)) within 1g for all realistic x.
Tier boundary: qualityTierForScore(roundScore(s)) is monotonic in s and stable across the float representations of 79.995, 85.995, 86.0.
11.2 Integration
Real MySQL in a container, not a mock. Per test: intents.create twice with the same key and body returns the same intent; same key with a different body returns GS-PAY-1001; concurrent duplicate creates produce exactly one row (run 20 concurrent requests, assert COUNT = 1); webhook with a valid signature credits once; the same webhook delivered five times credits once; a webhook with an invalid signature returns 401 and writes no transaction; a Casso callback with a valid token but a failing API re-fetch does not allocate.
11.3 Contract tests
Pact-style against recorded provider fixtures for PayOS and Casso, including at least one real sandbox capture per provider. Include the awkward shapes: Casso when as date-only, PayOS description truncated, amount as string vs number, missing counterAccountNumber.
11.4 Chaos
Kill the outbox consumer mid-batch → on restart, no event is lost and none is processed twice (assert handler-level idempotency counters).
Database connection drop during allocation → invoice paidMinor matches the allocation sum afterwards.
Provider webhook delivered while the matching consumer is down → transaction persists, matches on recovery.
Clock skew: provider timestamp one hour in the future → no crash, no reordering.
11.5 End-to-end (Playwright)
Create lot → issue invoice → display QR with memo token → simulate PayOS webhook at 90% of the amount → assert partially_paid and the correct aging bucket → simulate the remainder → assert paid, invoice.settled emitted once, aging bucket empty, and the lot detail timeline shows both allocations.
11.6 Load
100 webhooks/second for 60 seconds against the matching path. Assert p99 webhook response < 2s (the provider timeout budget) and zero duplicate allocations. Bank settlement batches arrive in bursts; a matching engine that is fine at 1/s and falls over at 50/s will fail on its first busy Friday.


12. Security, Privacy & Compliance + NEW
12.1 Threat model, briefly
The valuable attacks here are: forging a Casso webhook to mark an invoice paid (mitigated by ADR-03's API re-fetch); replaying a legitimate webhook to double-credit (mitigated by the (provider, providerTxnId) unique index); exfiltrating farmer bank details (§12.2); and uploading a malicious file through document intake (§12.3).
12.2 Bank details and PDPD
Vietnam's Decree 13/2023 (PDPD) treats an individual's financial account information as sensitive personal data, requiring explicit consent, purpose limitation, and a record of processing. Since many counterparties are individual farmers:

Encrypt bankAccountNumberEnc with AES-256-GCM using a KMS-managed key, per-row IV. Store bankAccountLast4 in plaintext for display.
Decryption is available only to ops_manager and platform_admin, and every decryption writes an access-log row — mirroring the sampleAccessLogs pattern already used for retained samples.
Consent capture on counterparty onboarding, with a consentedAt timestamp and the consent text version.
Confirm data-residency requirements with counsel before choosing a storage region. Flag as R2 below.
12.3 Document upload
Content-type allowlist (image/jpeg, image/png, image/heic, application/pdf); 25MB cap; presigned URLs expiring in 15 minutes; ClamAV or equivalent scan before OCR, with scanStatus = infected quarantining the document and alerting; never serve an uploaded file from the application origin — use a separate domain or signed CDN URLs to avoid stored-XSS via SVG or crafted PDF.
12.4 Secrets
WEBHOOK_SECRET, PAYOS_CHECKSUM_KEY, CASSO_API_KEY are provided by the secret manager, never in .env outside local dev. Add a CI check that fails the build if a high-entropy string matching the provider key formats appears in a diff.

13.2 Structured logging
Every webhook logs { requestId, provider, providerTxnId, signatureValid, amountMinor, currency, matchStatus, latencyMs }. 13.3 Nightly reconciliation
A job asserts, for every invoice: paidMinor == SUM(non-reversed allocations); SUM(allocations per transaction) <= transaction.amountMinor; every matched transaction has at least one allocation; no allocation crosses currencies without an fxRate. 13.5 Rollback
Every migration ships with a tested down. Migrations are expand-then-contract: add columns nullable, backfill, switch reads, switch writes, drop old columns in a later release. No release both adds and removes a column that the previous version reads. Feature flags are the first line of rollback; migrations are the last.


14. Acceptance Criteria Δ CHANGED
Rewritten so each is independently observable and has a stated tolerance.

Aging is correct. Given invoices due 5, 40, 70 and 120 days ago with partial payments, ar.aging returns exactly the current/30/60/90/90+ split, computed in Asia/Ho_Chi_Minh, matching a hand-calculated fixture to the minor unit.
Webhook idempotency. The same PayOS payload delivered 10 times concurrently produces exactly one provider_transactions row, one allocation, and one invoice.settled event.
Casso is not trusted. A Casso callback with a valid secure-token but a stubbed API re-fetch that returns 404 leaves paidMinor unchanged and raises an exception-queue entry.
Underpayment. A transfer of 90% of the invoice total sets partially_paid, leaves the remainder in the original aging bucket, and emits no invoice.settled.
Overpayment. A transfer of 110% sets paid, emits invoice.overpaid, and leaves the excess visible as unallocated in the exception queue and in ar.summary.
Unmatched money is never invisible. A transfer with an unrecognised memo appears in the exception queue within 60s and is included in the AR summary as suspense, not omitted.
No money overflow. An invoice of ₫5,000,000,000 (≈$190k) round-trips through create, QR generation, webhook, allocation and display without loss.
OCR proposes, humans dispose. Uploading a lab report at 0.99 confidence creates a draft cupping form and no approved cuppingSessions row; the tier used for revenue share is unchanged until a qualified user submits the form, and panel enforcement still rejects a single-cupper Tier 2 session.
Idempotency key reuse is refused. Same key, different body returns GS-PAY-1001, not the original response.
Traceability. For any allocation, a single query resolves payment → invoice → order or contract → lot → partner, and the Lot Detail page renders that chain.
Flags work at runtime. Setting autoAllocation: false stops automatic allocation within 30s with no deploy and no restart.
The refactor is safe. All fourteen pre-existing verified flows in app/README.md pass unchanged after emitEvent is rewritten.
