Auctum Unified Marketing Strategy
One master brand. One strategy. Ratified identity reference: AUCTUM Identity Core (2026-08-21). Fact basis: the ecosystem synthesis brief and the platform GTM brief. No figure in this document appears outside those sources.
1. Executive Summary
Auctum is the master brand of a single coffee ecosystem: the Auctum Atelier (bespoke luxury lots, successor to AUDACIA), Auctum Trading (Vietnam-origin import house, successor to Karmic Circle Coffee Trading), the Auctum Ledger (verified B2B distribution platform, successor to Greensheet), Flavor Foundry by Auctum (custom-fermentation ordering), and LotSpace by Auctum (the social trade network). All legacy names retire as brands and survive only as product labels under endorsement — “by Auctum” at 40–60% optical size. This strategy markets one company, not five.
The doctrine is the positioning: value is co-created, not extracted; verified, from origin. Auctum does not sell “specialty coffee”; it sells verified origin. The master tagline is “Auctum. Value is co-created, not extracted.” The trade lockup is “Auctum. Verified, from origin.” The luxury register speaks “The will to dare. The discipline to prove.” The verification mark — AUCTUM VERIFIED ORIGIN — appears only on lots that have passed the full verification stack. The seal is earned, never decorative.
The strategy unifies five proven go-to-market systems under one roof:
- The Audacia B2B playbook (Sample Box at $35–55 unit cost, 30–60 high-alignment accounts in 12–24 months, segments A/B/C) becomes the Auctum trade demand-generation motion.
- The 88-roaster validated call list (18 three-star, 67 two-star, 3 one-star) becomes the Auctum Trading outbound spine.
- The Greensheet campaign system (four positioning pillars, COF-001–005 automation, “Give a Kit, Get a Bag” referral engine, north-star Weekly Transacting Roasters) becomes the Auctum Ledger growth engine.
- The Cold Front Robusta campaign (four pillars: Built for Ice, No Apologies, Paid Like It Matters, Cup or Quit) becomes the Auctum consumer-culture program — disciplined punk, run in the Volume of the Counter, with “Operation”-military framing retired from public language.
- The thought-leadership stack — the Economic Autopsy (now the Auctum Autopsy QR object), the economic challenge manifesto, the Six Strategies to Protect Subsistence, and THE ORIGIN magazine tradition — becomes the press and category-creation layer.
One promise binds every channel: we do not ask you to believe us; we hand you the documents. Every number in every asset is ledgered, every price named, every farmer named.
2. Market Context
The trust deficit. Specialty coffee asks buyers to pay for virtue they cannot see. Certifications are table stakes; the industry’s own doctrine document argues the audit must shift from the sticker to the ledger — “verify the ledger, not the sticker.” Auctum’s category is verified green-coffee trade: every lot published with auditable, lot-level data (cup score, process, elevation, varietal, traceability, ESG profile) and every payment carrying an itemized receipt.
The commodity paradox. Coffee supports up to 25 million farming households producing 80% of world output (FAO, as cited in the corpus), yet the dominant pricing reference — the C-market — floats free of the cost of staying alive. In Vietnam, more than 95% of farmers sell only through roughly 2,000–3,000 collectors, across approximately 640,000 smallholdings, most under 2 hectares. The Auctum answer is structural: a $3.00/lb True Price Floor built from five documented pillars ($1.20 household subsistence, $0.80 farm operations, $0.20 debt service, $0.30 infrastructure, $0.50 resilience and reinvestment), held independent of the C-market. When the market falls, the floor does not.
The verification gap. Buyers cannot distinguish a verified lot from a story. Auctum closes the gap with an operational stack: a cupping SOP (every coffee cupped three times and averaged, panel of three or more mandatory for financial decisions, Agtron 55–60 standard roast), warehouse runbooks (±1.5% weight variance trigger, 11.0–12.5% moisture range), evidence-grade retained samples, a hash-chained audit ledger, exception tiers with SLAs, and the Auctum Autopsy — a QR object on every bag and menu that itemizes the lot’s economics. If you did not write it down, it did not happen.
The climate argument. Robusta is not the climate safety net the industry assumes. The Kath et al. 2020 study (Global Change Biology; ten years, 798 Southeast Asian farms) found optimal mean annual temperature below 20.5°C — versus the assumed 22–30°C — with each +1°C above optimum costing roughly 14% of yield, and yields collapsing 50% at 25.1°C and above. The corpus uses this to frame the $0.50/lb resilience pillar not as a premium but as a biological requirement.
Vietnam as the emerging specialty origin — the three-species frontier. Auctum Trading’s founding proof is Vietnam, worked across three species frontiers: Fine Robusta from Toi Nguyen’s Future Farm (realized farmgate $5.70/lb at CQI 82.75); high-altitude Java Arabica from Hieu Vuong and the Vuong family at Lac Duong, Lâm Đồng (1,360–1,520 masl, 12 hectares, 5,000 Java trees; realized farmgate $8.00/lb at CQI 86); and the Liberica revival led by Hana Choi and the 96B Project in Ho Chi Minh City. The brand is origin-principles-first — wherever the floor holds — with Vietnam as the documented founding proof.
Market-size context (directional, to re-verify before public use). The corpus cites specialty coffee at $111.5B (2025) growing to $251.7B by 2033 at 10.8% CAGR, with an alternate citation near $112B (2026) to $263B by 2035 at 9.9% CAGR, and up to 80% of consumers willing to pay more for ethically sourced coffee. These figures derive from mixed-quality market aggregators and are flagged in the identity canon as internal-only pending re-verification; they appear in this strategy for planning context and must be re-verified before any public claim.
Regulatory tailwind. EUDR compliance (large operators 30 December 2025, small operators by mid-2026; penalties up to 4% of EU turnover; polygon geo-coordinates for plots) converts traceability from a virtue into a license to operate — and the Auctum Ledger already treats EUDR geo-mapping and verification as product features.
3. Audiences
Auctum speaks to five audience families, each with a governed register. The truth never changes; the volume does.
B2B roasters — four profiles (from the Karmic Circle brochure §07, now the Auctum Trading ideal-customer set).
- Growing small-to-medium roasters who need verified supply and consistency without a sourcing department.
- Novelty seekers hunting new origins, processes, and species — the natural home of the three-species frontier and Flavor Foundry custom lots.
- Range expanders broadening their green program — Fine Robusta, Java Arabica, and Liberica microlots as differentiated menu additions.
- Logistics-averse roasters who want origin access without import operations — served by Auctum Trading’s US warehouse and forward-buying models (In-Network 50% deposit pre-order; Out-of-Network logistics assist).
Within these profiles, the platform segments demand as micro (1–5 staff, 150–400 lbs/mo), boutique (2–15 staff, 800–2,500 lbs/mo, dedicated green buyer), and commercial (5,000+ lbs/mo, procurement teams, ESG reporting). Register: the Volume of the Ledger — warm, exact, collegial; every claim carries a number.
Supply side — importers, exporters, and cooperatives (the Auctum Ledger’s paying customers). Exporters and cooperatives at origin (5–40 containers/yr) map to the $499/mo Exporter tier; mid-size import houses (10–60 active lots) to the $1,499/mo Import House tier; enterprise multi-origin traders to Enterprise Trading Co. from $4,000/mo. Register: the Volume of the Ledger, with Verification-register artifacts (runbooks, contracts, receipts) as proof.
Luxury and atelier clients (the Auctum Atelier, segments from the Audacia B2B strategy). Segment A: independent specialty cafés (1–5 locations, 50–200 kg/mo). Segment B: independent and micro roasters (500–5,000 kg/mo production). Segment C: specialty retail and hospitality. These buyers commission bespoke lots, co-designed with the atelier: joint sensory evaluation, farm visits, real-time audio/video feedback, and on-site harvest guidance led by Co-Founder Rae Tran. Register: the Volume of the House — lexicographic luxury; restraint as signal.
Producers and collectors. Never marketed at. The binding register rule: no punk at origin — the brand never markets at farmers with revolution rhetoric; it pays them transparently. Farmers’ real names and real prices always; no anonymous farmer-as-prop imagery. Producers encounter the brand through the Revenue Share Agreement, the True Price Receipt, White-Glove Services, and LotSpace farmer Spaces. Register: the Verification Register — flat, imperative, binding — plus the educator’s patience of the House voice in person.
Consumers (the Volume of the Counter). Reached through LotSpace by Auctum (taste identity, follows, Tip the Farmer, the Brave Few leaderboard) and the Cold Front Robusta campaign. Disciplined punk: punch at behavior, never people; wit with receipts. Consumers convert through the “Cup or Quit” blind-cupping challenge, cold-brew product ladder, and the Auctum Autopsy QR on the bag.
4. Positioning and Messaging House
Roof: Value is co-created, not extracted. Verified, from origin.
Category statement: Auctum is the verification-backed, subsistence-first coffee house: luxury atelier, origin importer, and trade platforms on one ledger of proof. We do not sell specialty coffee; we sell verified origin.
Pillar 1 — The Floor. Subsistence-first economics. The $3.00/lb True Price Floor, built from five documented pillars ($1.20 / $0.80 / $0.20 / $0.30 / $0.50), with specialty floors negotiated at $3.50–$4.00/lb, independent of the C-market. Proof points: realized farmgates of $5.70/lb for Fine Robusta (CQI 82.75) and $8.00/lb for Java Arabica (CQI 86); the revenue-share ladder of 50/35/20/10/0% of Net Sale Proceeds by cup-score tier; floor payments within 3/5/7 business days by partner tier, never clawed back except confirmed fraud. Key message: “Securing a farmer’s livelihood is not charity — it is the precondition for everything else worth having.”
Pillar 2 — The Proof. The verification stack. Cupping SOP, warehouse runbooks, retained-sample evidence chain, hash-chained ledger, True Price Receipts, and the Auctum Autopsy QR. Proof points: ≥80% of revenue share passed through to identified farmers (collector margin capped at 20%); the worked example — an 84-point lot returning $3.42/lb total ($3,420 on a 1,000-lb lot); every blind-cupping result published, win or lose. Key message: “We don’t ask you to believe us; we hand you the documents. If you did not write it down, it did not happen.”
Pillar 3 — The Craft. Fermentation science leadership. Twenty-five years of Hawaiian R&D lineage (Paradise Roasters, Isla Customs, R. Miguel), the 28-recipe Fermentation Cookbook (2nd edition), the Flavor Primitives library (~12 codified protocols at v1, nine control dials), and Flavor Foundry by Auctum (HOUSE minimum 5 bags/300 kg; SEMI minimum 10 bags/600 kg; DE NOVO research lots at 6–18 months). Proof point: yeast-inoculated ferments typically add 0.5–1.5 cup-score points over wild-ferment controls. Key message: “Each lot is an argument for what coffee can become.”
Pillar 4 — The Circle. The community of the brave. Named farmers, named prices, real receipts: Toi Nguyen, Hieu Vuong and the Vuong family, Hana Choi. The Brave Few early-adopter program runs unchanged across buyers, cafés, and consumers. Key message: “Culture doesn’t shift by chasing the masses. It shifts by empowering the few brave enough to go first.”
Per-audience message map (register → message → proof).
- Roasters (Ledger register): “We paid $5.70/lb for a bean you’re not supposed to respect. Imagine what respect costs.” Proof: lot-level economics, the Autopsy QR, unrounded cup scores.
- Supply side (Ledger register): “The offer sheet, elevated to an instrument.” Proof: verified lot catalog, COF-001–005 automation, hash-chained audit trail, EUDR geo-mapping support.
- Atelier clients (House register): “The will to dare. The discipline to prove.” Proof: 25 years of R&D lineage, bespoke co-design, competition-grade lots.
- Producers and collectors (Verification register): “Subsistence is a non-negotiable base, akin to a living wage.” Proof: the floor, the 3/5/7-day SLA, the True Price Receipt, ≥80% pass-through.
- Consumers (Counter register): “Your coffee doesn’t need a pinky. It needs ice.” Proof: the $5.70 farmgate, the $3.00 floor, Cup or Quit blind results published either way.
Language discipline (binding). Say lot, cup score, process, offer, position — never product, rating, method. Scientific names italicized (Coffea canephora, Coffea liberica, Saccharomyces cerevisiae). Vietnamese diacritics preserved exactly (Đắk Lắk, Lâm Đồng). “Operation”-military framing is retired from public language; internal codenames may persist. Banned in Counter voice: “elevated,” “curated,” “journey,” “ritual,” “premium” as self-description. Never say “most ethical,” “saving farmers,” or “buy us and feel good.” Avoid “sustainable,” “ethical” (without data), and “farm-to-cup.”
5. Channel Strategy
5.1 B2B demand generation (Auctum Trading + the Atelier)
- The Sample Box program (successor to the Audacia Sample Box): 2–3 single origins at 100 g each — never a blend, named farm and farmer — with Farm Origin Brief cards answering the three reproducibility questions in actual figures, a Framework Card of 200 words or fewer (“Most ethical coffee labels tell you about process. We tell you about economics.”), a password-tracked QR Digital Origin Report, and a transparent pricing sheet (true cost, subsistence floor, surplus, farmgate, logistics, margin, suggested retail). Unit cost target $35–55; 50–80 boxes per cycle; qualification gate (specialty program, provenance interest, ≥50 kg/mo potential, no competing exclusive contract). Funnel: 150–200 curated targets → the warm question (“How do you currently verify that your coffee farms are economically viable?”) → box → proposal → onboarding pack; conversion targets 30% → 40% → 50%, approximately 9 new accounts per 150 contacts.
- The 88-roaster tiered call list: 18 three-star roasters contacted first (fermentation and processing-innovation aligned; anchor archetype Paradise Roasters), 67 two-star in the second wave, 3 one-star watchlist. Guardrails: confirm decision-maker level with FairWave collective members (Black & White, Ceremony, Messenger) and the PT’s/Bird Rock ownership group; list data is “verified as of August 2026” and must be re-verified at Phase 2 launch.
- The four-pillar social series, re-voiced to Auctum: POS-01 “Value Before Tasting,” POS-02 “Price Isn’t the Whole Story,” POS-03 “Coffee Is Infrastructure, Not a Product,” POS-04 “Reinvestment > Extraction.” Codes stay internal; the public series runs under Auctum pillar names. Channel cadence per the legacy kit: LinkedIn long-form, Instagram carousels, TikTok/Shorts scripts, Twitter threads, newsletter; four 60-second anchor videos with 15-second cutdowns; the iceberg infographic (“your coffee is an iceberg”); a four-week posting calendar. Channel targets carried from the kit: 25k+ LinkedIn impressions per post, 60% video-through rate, 8%+ IG saves, 3–5% CTR to site, 2–3% social-to-demo.
5.2 Platform growth (the Auctum Ledger)
- Pricing architecture: Roaster side — Cupper (free), Roaster Pro $49/mo, Roaster Business $149/mo. Supply side — Exporter $499/mo, Import House $1,499/mo, Enterprise Trading Co. from $4,000/mo. Transactions carry a 1.0–1.5% payments/escrow take rate on GMV. Never advertising-based monetization.
- Sample-kit program: curated kits shipped to prospect roasters; maximum 2 active kits per roaster; kit snapshots lot pricing at assembly as an anti-bait-and-switch guarantee. COF-001 reference unit economics: $38/kit.
- COF-001–005 automation engine: trigger/condition/action lifecycle — kit delivered plus 4 days sends Touch-1; feedback rating ≥ 4 sends pricing; rating ≤ 2 escalates to CRM plus consultative SMS; pricing-link click triggers volume-discount CTA; first order halts nurture and enrolls onboarding. Campaign codes remain internal taxonomy.
- Referral engine: “Give a Kit, Get a Bag” — target K-factor ≥ 0.6 (modeled at 0.61 with a 2.56× LTV multiplier), behind a fraud stack.
- Lifecycle discipline: blended CAC target ≤ $250 (hard cap $500); LTV:CAC ≥ 3:1; Cox proportional-hazard churn model with high-risk threshold ≥ 0.70 triggering auto-assigned CSM interventions (email campaign, sales call, discount offer, survey) with save-rate tracking. Every campaign change ships behind a Bayesian A/B experiment (confidence threshold 0.95).
- North star: Weekly Transacting Roasters (WTR). The LotSpace rule applies ecosystem-wide: no engagement metric enters the board deck unless it maps to a revenue event.
5.3 Atelier (the Auctum Atelier)
- Bespoke commissions: co-designed custom lots under the Atelier partnership model — joint sensory evaluation, farm visits, real-time audio/video feedback, on-site harvest guidance. Growth by depth, not breadth: 30–60 high-alignment accounts in 12–24 months across segments A/B/C; Year-1 targets 25–40 active accounts, 20%+ sample-box conversion, 5 co-branding roasters, NPS 50+, 10k LinkedIn impressions per month.
- Harvest-season correspondence model: the atelier’s editorial rhythm follows origin harvest calendars (Arabica November–March; Robusta October–late February; Liberica February–April), converting real-time harvest feedback into correspondence with commissioning clients — the House register’s equivalent of a drop calendar.
- Competition and auction presence: competition-grade and auction-grade lots position the Atelier at the market’s ceiling; the corpus’s auction reference point is the Best of Panama 2025 washed Geisha world record at $30,204/kg, with 30 of 50 lots above $1,000/kg (directional figure; re-verify before public use).
